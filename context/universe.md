# 🔑 LEGEND

Every book carries **one TIER** and **one WEIGHT**. Flags appear only when they apply.

### ① TIER — *How much does this matter?*

*A heat scale: red is hot, blue is cool. The colour **is** the priority.*

|              | Tier                | Colour code         | What it means                                                                       |
| :----------: | ------------------- | ------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **🔴** | **CORE**      | Red ·`#E03131`   | **Read it.** If I only ever read the CORE books, the domain is covered.       |
| **🟡** | **DEEPEN**    | Amber ·`#F08C00` | **Read after the CORE** — and only when this domain is actually live for me. |
| **🔵** | **REFERENCE** | Blue ·`#1C7ED6`  | **Never cover-to-cover.** Open it when I have the specific problem.           |

> 💡 *The hex codes are there so this maps cleanly onto Notion tags, Obsidian callouts, or a spreadsheet column without me having to invent a palette twice.*

### ② WEIGHT — *What does it cost me at 30 min/day?*

*My real constraint. A 900-page book and a 120-page book are not the same decision.*

|                | Weight           | Roughly                                          |
| :-------------: | ---------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| **`S`** | **Short**  | One sitting to one week · under ~200 pages      |
| **`M`** | **Medium** | Two to three weeks · ~200–400 pages            |
| **`L`** | **Long**   | A month or more · 400+ pages, or dense and slow |

### ③ FLAGS — *What I need to know before opening it*

|                | Flag                   | What it means                                                                                                                                      |
| :------------: | ---------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **⚠️** | **Caveat**       | The book is real and worth reading, but something in it is contested, outdated, or overstated. The specific issue is named inline — never hidden. |
| **♾️** | **Lifelong**     | Not meant to be finished. Returned to for years, a few pages at a time. Do not apply my completion instinct to these.                              |
|  **🆓**  | **Free**         | Full text legally available online. No cost, no excuse.                                                                                            |
| **🇳🇵** | **My ground**    | Directly about Nepal, the Newars, or South Asia.                                                                                                   |
|  **↔**  | **Cross-listed** | The full entry lives in another cluster. Read once, counts twice.                                                                                  |

## ⚙️ DOMAIN 1 — SELF-COMMAND · `self-command`

### 1.1 · Why I Procrastinate · `why-i-procrastinate`

> *Diagnosis before tactics. Skipping this is why discipline advice has never stuck.*

- 🟡 `S` **Solving the Procrastination Puzzle** — Timothy Pychyl — *Short, research-based, zero fluff. Pychyl is the academic who established procrastination as an emotion-regulation problem.*
- 🟡 `S` **The War of Art** — Steven Pressfield — *Names the enemy "Resistance." One hour, quotable, useful on a bad morning.*
- 🔵 `M` **Do Hard Things** — Steve Magness — *Real toughness vs. self-punishment. The antidote if discipline starts feeling like penance.*

### 1.2 · Habits & Systems · `habits-and-systems`

- 🔵 `M` **The Power of Habit** — Charles Duhigg — *More journalism than method. Skip unless I want the stories.*

### 1.3 · Attention, Dopamine & Digital Discipline · `attention-dopamine-and-digital-discipline`

> *My named momentum-killer — it appears three separate times in my own dataset. This cluster also covers compulsive use of any high-dopamine input, which is the honest home for the "sexual energy" question my hierarchy raises.*

- 🔴 `M` **Deep Work** — Cal Newport — *How to actually get and protect the 2–4 hour blocks I say I want.*
- 🟡 `S` **Digital Minimalism** — Cal Newport — *The 30-day declutter protocol. Do it, don't just read it.*
- 🟡 `M` **Indistractable** — Nir Eyal — *Internal triggers + timeboxing. A practical counterweight to Newport's absolutism.*
- 🔵 `M` **Stolen Focus** — Johann Hari — *Systemic argument — attention as something taken, not lost. Good context, thin on action.*

### 1.4 · Energy, Rest & Sustainable Effort · `energy-rest-and-sustainable-effort`

- 🔵 `M` ⚠️ **The Willpower Instinct** — Kelly McGonigal — *Built substantially on ego-depletion research that has failed replication. The exercises are still useful; the underlying theory should be held loosely.*

## 💪 DOMAIN 3 — BODY & ENERGY · `body-and-energy`

### 3.1 · Sleep & Circadian Rhythm · `sleep-and-circadian-rhythm`

> *The 4:30–5:00am wake I want is only sustainable if this side is solved first.*

- 🔴 `L` ⚠️ **Why We Sleep** — Matthew Walker — *The best single case for sleep as the foundation of everything else.*
  > *⚠️ A detailed public critique (Alexey Guzey, 2019) documented specific overstated and misattributed claims, and Walker has issued corrections. The central message — that chronic short sleep is genuinely damaging — remains well supported. Treat individual statistics as directional, not precise.*
  >
- 🔵 `M` **The Circadian Code** — Satchin Panda — *Light timing and eating windows, from a circadian researcher. Reference-grade.*

### 3.2 · Strength, Movement & Physical Confidence · `strength-movement-and-physical-confidence`

- 🔴 `L` **Bigger Leaner Stronger** — Michael Matthews — *One complete, evidence-based program for a male lifter. Follow it and stop reading about training — the reading is the procrastination.*
- 🟡 `M` **Built to Move** — Kelly & Juliet Starrett — *Ten mobility tests and the work to pass them. Directly serves "posture as confidence" and desk damage.*
- 🔵 `L` **Starting Strength** — Mark Rippetoe — *Technique reference for the main barbell lifts. Consult chapters; don't read it through.*
- 🔵 `M` **Endure** — Alex Hutchinson — *Where physical limits actually come from — mostly the brain. Good if my stamina concern is real.*

### 3.3 · Nutrition, Fasting & Longevity · `nutrition-fasting-and-longevity`

- 🔴 `L` **Outlive** — Peter Attia — *Healthspan over lifespan. The serious version of my "longevity mindset" — a framework and a set of markers, not a diet.*
- 🟡 `M` **The Hungry Brain** — Stephan Guyenet — *Why eating goes wrong at the brain level. Removes moral judgement from food, which matters given how I talk to myself.*
- 🔵 `M` **The Complete Guide to Fasting** — Jason Fung — *"Fasting protocols" from my hierarchy, with the physiology. Fung is a nephrologist with strong views; read the mechanism, hold the certainty loosely.*

> 💡 *Context note: nearly all nutrition books assume a Western food environment. Dal-bhat, seasonal Nepali produce, and a South Asian metabolic profile all change the specifics. Take the principles — protein sufficiency, fibre, whole foods, meal timing — and rebuild the plan locally.*

### 3.4 · Breath, Nervous System & Recovery · `breath-nervous-system-and-recovery`

- 🔴 `M` ⚠️ **Breath** — James Nestor — *Excellent and genuinely useful on nasal breathing, CO₂ tolerance and slow breathing. Some historical and physiological claims are stretched for narrative effect — take the practices, hold the story loosely.*
- 🟡 `M` **The Oxygen Advantage** — Patrick McKeown — *More rigorous, less readable. The technical companion to Nestor.*
- 🟡 `M` **Burnout** — Emily & Amelia Nagoski — *"Completing the stress cycle" — the mechanism nearly every productivity book skips. Removing the stressor is not the same as discharging the stress.*
- 🔵 `L` **Light on Pranayama** — B.K.S. Iyengar — *My hierarchy names pranayama specifically. This is the primary source, not a modern repackaging of it.*

### 3.5 · Yoga as Awareness · `yoga-as-awareness`

> *My hierarchy says "yoga as awareness, not fitness." These are the books that treat it that way.*

- 🟡 `L` **Light on Yoga** — B.K.S. Iyengar — *The standard reference. Asana with precision and sequencing.*
- 🟡 `S` **The Heart of Yoga** — T.K.V. Desikachar — *Yoga adapted to the person, not the person to the yoga. The philosophy that separates practice from performance.*

## 🧩 DOMAIN 4 — THINKING, LEARNING & DECISIONS · `thinking-learning-and-decisions`

### 4.1 · How to Actually Learn · `how-to-actually-learn`

> *The direct fix for my own sentence: "I mostly never go in depth, but shallow-learn everything quickly."*

- 🔴 `M` **Ultralearning** — Scott Young — *Aggressive, project-based depth on a deadline. Nine principles, built around going absurdly deep on one thing instead of thinly across ten.*
- 🔴 `M` **Make It Stick** — Brown, Roediger & McDaniel — *Why rereading and highlighting feel productive and don't work. Retrieval practice, spacing, interleaving. This will measurably improve my AI reading pipeline — especially the comprehension-check stage.*

- 🔵 `M` **A Mind for Numbers** — Barbara Oakley — *Focused vs. diffuse mode. Useful concept, thin book.*

### 4.2 · Mental Models & Systems Thinking · `mental-models-and-systems-thinking`

- 🔴 `S` **Thinking in Systems** — Donella Meadows — *Stocks, flows, feedback loops, leverage points. My hierarchy says "systems thinking" five times without ever defining it. This defines it — and it will change how I read my own life document.*
- 🟡 `L` ⚠️ **Thinking, Fast and Slow** — Daniel Kahneman — *Foundational on cognitive bias. The priming and social-priming chapters did not replicate, and Kahneman publicly acknowledged that section was built on too-weak evidence. The System 1/System 2 frame and the prospect-theory work stand.*
- 🟡 `S` **The Great Mental Models, Vol. 1** — Shane Parrish — *Compact and well-chosen. One model per session.*
- 🔵 `L` **Poor Charlie's Almanack** — Charlie Munger — *The multidisciplinary latticework, plus the psychology-of-misjudgement talk. Browse; don't march.*

### 4.3 · Deciding Under Uncertainty · `deciding-under-uncertainty`

> *Built specifically for an over-researcher who wants certainty before acting.*

- 🟡 `M` **How to Decide** — Annie Duke — *The workbook version. Actual usable tools.*
- 🟡 `M` **Decisive** — Chip & Dan Heath — *The WRAP framework. Practical and immediately applicable.*
- 🔵 `M` **Superforecasting** — Philip Tetlock — *Calibrated prediction from a twenty-year forecasting tournament. Fascinating; less directly applicable.*

## 🧘 DOMAIN 13 — PHILOSOPHY, MEANING & SPIRITUALITY · `philosophy-meaning-and-spirituality`

> **My own filter, sharpened.** I named several modern motivational speakers as fraudulent, and named Krishnamurti and Adi Shankaracharya as people I don't know enough about. That instinct is worth keeping and worth making explicit:
> *Be suspicious of anyone selling certainty, charisma, a personal brand, or a course. Be open to primary texts, and to teachers whose central instruction is to verify things for yourself.*
> This domain is built entirely on that filter — and where a teacher failed his own standard, it's stated in the entry.

### 13.1 · Stoicism & Practical Philosophy · `stoicism-and-practical-philosophy`

- 🔴 `S` ♾️ 🆓 **Meditations** — Marcus Aurelius — *A Roman emperor's private notebook, never intended for publication — which is precisely why it's trustworthy. A few pages at a time, for years. The ideal slow companion for my temperament.*
  > 💡 *The translation matters enormously. **Gregory Hays** (Modern Library) is the readable modern standard and is worth buying. Older public-domain translations (Long, Farquharson) are free but noticeably stiffer.*
  >
- 🟡 `M` **Letters from a Stoic** — Seneca — *Warmer and more practical than Marcus. Read as individual letters, never as a book.*
- 🟡 `M` 🆓 **Discourses and Selected Writings** — Epictetus — *The dichotomy of control at its source, from a former slave. Free in older translations.*
- 🔵 `L` **The Practicing Stoic** — Ward Farnsworth — *Organised by theme, with the original passages alongside. The best single reference for the school.*
- 🔵 `M` **A Guide to the Good Life** — William Irvine — *A modern systematic introduction, for when I want the framework before the fragments.*

### 13.2 · Meaning, Mortality & the Absurd · `meaning-mortality-and-the-absurd`

- 🔴 `S` **Man's Search for Meaning** — Viktor Frankl — *A psychiatrist's account of the camps, and the argument that meaning is found rather than given, and survives conditions that destroy everything else. Short and permanently altering. Serves my Level 1 work on suffering and death directly.*
- 🟡 `S` **The Myth of Sisyphus** — Albert Camus — *"Camus' absurd courage" is in my hierarchy. This is the source. Read the first essay and the final pages; the middle is literary criticism.*
- 🟡 `S` **When Breath Becomes Air** — Paul Kalanithi — *A neurosurgeon's memoir of dying at 37. My hierarchy lists "death contemplation" as a practice; this makes it real rather than theoretical.*
- 🔵 `M` **Being Mortal** — Atul Gawande — *Aging, medicine, and what a good ending actually requires. Practical as well as philosophical — and relevant to my parents, not only to me.*
- 🔵 `L` **The Denial of Death** — Ernest Becker — *Pulitzer winner. How mortality-denial quietly drives ambition, culture and the need to be exceptional. Heavy, and uncomfortably close to "perfect in everything."*

### 13.3 · Vedanta & Non-Duality · `vedanta-and-non-duality`

- 🔴 `M` ♾️ **The Bhagavad Gita** *(Eknath Easwaran translation)* — *Action without attachment to fruit, dharma, duty — the exact concepts sitting in my Level 1. Easwaran's introduction and chapter prefaces make it genuinely readable without diluting it.*
- 🟡 `L` ♾️ **The Upanishads** *(Easwaran)* — *The philosophical root beneath the Gita. Older, stranger, less prescriptive.*
- 🟡 `M` **Vivekachudamani / Self-Knowledge** — Adi Shankaracharya *(Nikhilananda or Chinmayananda edition)* — *I named Shankaracharya myself as someone I don't know enough about. Unassisted Shankara is hard going — use a commentaried edition, not a bare translation.*

- 🔵 `S` **The Book: On the Taboo Against Knowing Who You Are** — Alan Watts — *A clear Western entry point to non-duality. An explainer, not a substitute for the sources.*

### 13.4 · Buddhism & Newar Heritage · `buddhism-and-newar-heritage`

> 🇳🇵 *My own inheritance. The Kathmandu Valley preserved a living Mahayana and Vajrayana tradition after Buddhism had largely disappeared from the rest of South Asia — which makes Newar Buddhism genuinely unusual, and almost entirely unknown to the people who grew up inside it.*

- 🔵 `L` 🇳🇵 **Monk, Householder, and Tantric Priest** — David N. Gellner *(Cambridge, 1992)* — *A rigorous ethnography of Newar Mahayana and Vajrayana Buddhism, based on fieldwork in Lalitpur. ### ❓ Where I'm less certain, and you should check before committing

- **Page counts and my `S`/`M`/`L` weights** are estimates, not measurements. Edition and translation change them substantially. Verify before starting a `L`.
- **The 🆓 flags** reflect works in the public domain or released free by their authors. For *Meditations*, *Crime and Punishment*, the *Gita* and the *Dhammapada*, **the free versions are older translations** — the specific modern translations I recommend (Hays, Easwaran) are not free. That distinction is real and I've noted it inline.
- **Thomas Bell's *Kathmandu*** — I'm confident it exists and is well regarded, but I did not verify its details this session. Treat it as a lead, not a citation.
- **Availability in Nepal** — several academic titles (Slusser, Gellner, Stiller) are expensive or out of print. Check the Internet Archive, Mandala Book Point, Pilgrims, Thuprai and Booksmandala before assuming they're out of reach.

# P2b — the book page: the author, and the book then and now

**Stage 2b. Once per book, after `/scaffold` and before chapter one. Search on.**

**Length: 1806 words.** `node scripts/prompt-words.mjs` measures it; `--stamp` writes it back.

> **Why this exists.** He read a complete sample book end to end and asked for three things the
> pipeline had no producer for:
>
> > *"Every book must display its original release date, along with any major updates or version
> > dates (major changes only), shown somewhere in the presentation."*
> >
> > *"A more detailed section on the author's context: the time and surroundings they were born and
> > raised in, where they worked, and the experiences that led the author to think the way they do —
> > anything that could have influenced what the author says in the book/chapters."*
> >
> > *"The book's content was written in a particular time and may not be fully relevant today. This
> > section should surface: any changes the author themselves made later, counter-research from other
> > scholars, new/different circumstances, science-based counter-arguments, shifts in general
> > perception, or anything — research backing, similar findings from other scholars, or other
> > rational points — that supports or argues against the book's content/author, including things
> > that weren't relevant when the book was written but are relevant now."*
> >
> > — his instructions, 2026-09-14. `context/reader.md` §10b.
>
> **The dates are fields, and the sections are prose, so they are produced in two places.**
> `published` and `editions` go into `book.json` at `P2`, and the page chrome renders them on every
> page of the book. `## Author context` and `## Context then vs. context today` are sections of the
> book page, and this prompt writes them. `book-spec.md` §6 fixes the headings; `standards.md` §8 is
> the standard they are written to.

**Why it is not part of `P2`.** `P2` emits one JSON object that a script validates key by key.
Several thousand words of sourced biography have no key to live in, and a brief is agreed before the
book page exists to paste into. `/scaffold` creates the page with its fixed `###` already in place;
this prompt fills them.

## Paste with it

`00-CONTEXT-PACK` ● · the whole `book.json` ● · the table of contents and the opening or preface ● ·
`standards` ● · `source-rules` ● · `book-spec` ●. **Search on.** No chapter text beyond the opening:
this page places the book and its author; it does not pre-digest a chapter.

`GUIDE.md` Appendix A carries the same row.

---

````text
Here is book.json for [BOOK], its table of contents, and its opening
pages or preface. Write three sections of the book's page, and nothing
else:

  ## Author context
  ## Context then vs. context today
  ## Sources

This book is kind: [read | reference | lifelong]. Check that against
the "kind" field in book.json. If they disagree, say so and stop: the
kind decides which page this is.

WHAT YOU DO NOT WRITE

  · ## Why this book, now · ## Questions I brought to it · ## What it
    changed · ## Where I stopped, if I stopped. Those are mine. On a
    reference or lifelong page the same goes for ## What it is for,
    ## What it is, ## Problems I brought to it and ## The log.
  · Anything between the CHAPTERS markers. That grid is generated by a
    script, and the script discards whatever is written there.
  · THE DATES, AS TEXT ON THE PAGE. The first-published date and every
    major edition live only in book.json, as "published" and
    "editions". The site reads them from there and shows them under the
    title and beside the book's name on every chapter page. Typed into
    the page as well, they get a second place to disagree.
  · A summary of what the book argues. I read ## Author context before
    chapter one, so it describes a life, not the chapters' claims.

BUT CHECK THE DATES. Search for the original publication date and for
every MAJOR revision, and compare what you find with "published" and
"editions" in the book.json above.
  · A major revision is a revised edition with chapters added or
    removed, a substantially updated evidence base, or a new afterword
    that changes the argument. A paperback, a new cover or a corrected
    typo is not a version.
  · Acceptable sources: the publisher's page, a national library or
    WorldCat record, the copyright page, the new edition's own preface,
    or the author announcing the revision.
  · For a translated work, the translation I am reading is an edition.
    Its translator and year belong in "editions".
  · For a work older than print, "published" is the approximate
    composition date, written like "c. 170–180 CE", and the first
    printed edition goes in "editions".
  If book.json is wrong or incomplete, say so AFTER the fence: give me
  the exact JSON to change, with the source for each correction. Never
  correct it silently, and never put a date into the page to make up
  for it. If book.json matches, write one line after the fence:
  "book.json dates checked: match", with the sources you used.

## AUTHOR CONTEXT — four ### headings, these exact strings, this order

  ### Where and when they grew up
  ### What they studied and where they worked
  ### What led to this book
  ### What that means for reading it

  I asked for biography, in detail, so write it in detail:
  · When and where they were born and grew up, their family and
    schooling where it is documented, where they worked and what they
    did there, and what was happening in their field and in the world
    when they wrote this book.
  · EVERY FACT IS SOURCED. The author's own site, memoir or interviews
    are tier A for what they say about themselves. An encyclopaedia,
    an obituary or a published profile is tier B. The book's own jacket
    copy is never a source for facts about its author.
  · INNER LIFE ONLY WHERE THE AUTHOR OR A BIOGRAPHER SAYS SO. "Her
    father's absence made her distrust institutions" needs a source in
    which she or a biographer says exactly that. Anything else is
    interpretation: label it as interpretation, or cut it.
  · The last heading connects the life to the book: which features of
    the argument those circumstances help explain, one feature at a
    time. It NEVER uses them to explain the argument away. Where an
    author stood tells me why a claim looks the way it does. It never
    tells me the claim is wrong — that needs evidence, and it belongs
    in the next section.
  · Where little is documented — an ancient author, a private one —
    say how little, and do not fill the gap with plausible colour.

## CONTEXT THEN VS. CONTEXT TODAY — six ### headings, exact, in order

  ### The world the book was written in
  ### What the author has changed or said since
  ### Research since — what supports it and what does not
  ### What critics and other scholars argue
  ### What is different today
  ### What still holds

  THE RULE THAT MAKES IT TRUSTWORTHY IS SYMMETRY. Report what has
  SUPPORTED the book since publication — research, other scholars
  reaching a similar conclusion by a different route, predictions that
  held — with the same effort and the same sourcing as what has
  undermined it. A section that only finds problems is as manufactured
  as one that only finds praise.

  · DATE EVERY POINT. "Since publication" means nothing without a year.
  · TYPE EVERY POINT, in the sentence, as one of four kinds of change:
      the author changed their mind · the evidence changed ·
      the world changed · the audience's perception changed
    They have different consequences. A paragraph that mixes them lets
    a change in fashion read as a change in evidence.
  · Every empirical claim carries a strength word in the sentence:
    replicated · single study · contested · failed to replicate.
  · THE STANDING LIST IS CHECKED HERE, ONCE, so every chapter inherits
    the result: priming · ego depletion · power posing · learning
    styles · the 10,000-hour rule · handwriting beats typing. If the
    book makes one of those claims, look it up and pin it.
  · What critics argue is steelmanned: their strongest point, not the
    one you can answer. Do not rebut it in the paragraph that raises it.
  · NEVER SUBTRACT FROM THE BOOK. A claim that has aged badly is
    described here, dated, beside the book. You do not declare it
    withdrawn, and the chapters keep it in the author's words.
  · A book with no empirical claims — a novel, a scripture — says so in
    one sentence under the research heading, then covers how the book's
    reading has changed, which it does have.
  · ### What still holds ends in a :::tip[What still holds] block: what
    survives everything above, with its qualification. A version with
    no qualification did not survive the section; it skipped it.

  If what you find disagrees with "outsideView" in book.json, say so
  AFTER the fence and give me the rewritten three lines. The page is the
  authority and the digest is rewritten to match it.

THE STOPPING RULE — a number, not a feeling. Stop when all five hold:
  1. at least three independent sources, none of them the book, its
     publisher or the author's own site
  2. at least one is a critic — someone arguing the book is wrong, not
     someone noting it is imperfect
  3. at least one piece of independent support, or a sentence saying
     you looked for it and found none
  4. every standing-list claim the book makes has been looked up
  5. you can state the strongest objection in a sentence you would not
     be embarrassed to show its author
  CEILING: if 20 minutes of searching has produced no substantive
  critic, stop and write that down as the finding. An empty critics
  section is honest. A manufactured one is inherited by every chapter.

## SOURCES — | Claim | Source | Read on | Tier |

  One row for every date, every biographical fact and every claim in
  the two sections above. Source is an anchored URL, not a bare title.
  Read on is the date you opened it. Tier is A, B, C or D, and tier C
  supports only what a source claims about itself, never what is true.
  If you cannot verify something, write
    [UNVERIFIED: what it is attributed to; what I searched; what I
     found instead; what changes if it is wrong]
  with all four parts. An invented biography, date or study is the
  easiest thing you can produce and the hardest thing I can catch.

HOW TO WRITE IT — my standard: "The user must be able to interpret and
understand every sentence produced."

  · Complete sentences, not notes. No arrows, slash-lists or dropped
    articles in prose. One main idea per sentence.
  · Define every term the first time it appears — a field's jargon, a
    school of thought, a named debate — in plain words.
  · An example beside every claim that is not self-evident.
  · Keep the meaning intact. Simplifying may change the words. It may
    never change a claim's strength, scope or conditions. The test:
    would the source sign the simpler sentence?
  · Length is not a concern. Never shorten by compressing. Padding is
    still banned: preamble, recap, reassurance, and jacket copy such as
    "seminal" or "must-read".
  · No ## other than the three above, no #### anywhere, no emoji in a
    heading. Bare < and { in prose go in backticks. Internal links are
    root-absolute.

MISSING INPUTS — NAME THEM AND STOP

  If book.json is not above, stop: without "published" you cannot date
  "since publication", and without "kind" you cannot tell which page
  this is. If you do not have search, say so in the first line and
  stop — a biography and a research history written from memory are
  exactly the fluent, unsourced prose I cannot check. If a square
  bracket above is still unfilled, quote it back to me and stop.

FORMAT
  One four-backtick fence holding the three sections, in the order
  above, ready to paste over the TODOs. Nothing outside it, except the
  date check and any outsideView correction, after the fence.
````

## Then

Paste the fence into the book's `index.mdx`, **section by section**: `## Author context` and
`## Context then vs. context today` over the two stub sections of the same names, which sit together
near the top, and `## Sources` over the stub table at the foot. **Leave the `CHAPTERS` markers and
everything between them untouched.**

**Flip nothing.** The book page is not a draft — its template carries no `draft` field — so it is
compiled and checked on the next build. Run `/validate`.

If the date check found an error, edit `book.json`. **No flag is needed**: the dates are read at build
time, so editing the file is the whole amendment (`book-spec.md` §3c). If `outsideView` disagreed
with the page, rewrite those three lines to match the page.

**Re-running this later** — an edition appears, a critic publishes — change as few words as the
correction needs. His comments on the book page find their passage by its words, and rewording that
passage detaches them (`md-spec.md` §5d).

**When to read it.** `## Author context` before chapter one: it describes a life, not the chapter's
claims, and it is the context he asked to have in hand. `## Context then vs. context today` is best
read after the first chapter's recall, because criticism read first frames what he looks for in the
chapter. That is guidance, not a lock (`book-spec.md` §6).

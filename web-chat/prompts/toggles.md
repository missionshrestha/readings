# Module toggles

**Append one line to `P3`, `P4` or `P6` when a book calls for it.** They are additive and they cost
attention, so a toggle on every chapter is a toggle that has stopped meaning anything.

Each one below carries four things: **the line to paste**, **when it earns its cost**, **what it
looks like when it lands**, and **what it looks like when it did not**. The last column is the one
that matters — every toggle has a failure mode that produces output, and output is easy to mistake
for value.

**Two or three per book, not per chapter.** If you find yourself pasting the same toggle into every
chapter, it belongs in `P0` instead, once.

---

## Scripts

```
Include ready-to-use scripts and exact phrasings, natural for a Kathmandu context.
```

**Earns its cost** in communication, negotiation and leadership books — anywhere the gap is *"I know
what to say and not how to say it"*.

**Landed** when a script contains a sentence you would actually say out loud, with the hesitation
left in. **Did not** when every line is fluent, symmetrical and slightly American: *"I appreciate
you bringing that to me, and I want to make sure we're aligned."* That is a script for a book, not
for a room.

## Drills

```
Include specific practice drills I can run this week.
```

**Earns its cost** on speaking, social and physical skills — anything where the bottleneck is reps.

**Landed** when a drill names a duration, a frequency and a way to tell whether you did it. **Did
not** when it produces "practise active listening in your next three conversations" — an
instruction with no observable and no end.

## Reflection

```
Include reflection questions that would expose my blind spots.
```

**Earns its cost** in psychology, philosophy and relationships.

**Landed** when at least one question is uncomfortable to answer honestly. **Did not** when the
questions are the kind you can answer well: *"What does success look like for you?"* A reflection
question you enjoy answering is a prompt for a journal, not a probe.

## Cheat sheet

```
Include a one-page cheat sheet and comparison tables.
```

**Earns its cost** on frameworks, finance and reference books — the 96 🔵 tier especially.

**Landed** when it is genuinely usable without the chapter beside it. **Did not** when it is the
chapter's headings in a table. Test: cover the chapter and see whether the sheet still tells you
what to do.

## Decision tree

```
Convert this into IF-THEN rules and a Mermaid decision tree.
```

**Earns its cost** on anything procedural.

**Landed** when a branch has a condition you could evaluate in the moment without thinking about it.
**Did not** when the branches are *"if the situation is complex"* — which is a decision tree that
requires you to have already made the decision.

## Flags

```
Include red flags and green flags — in my behaviour and in others'.
```

**Earns its cost** on relationships, hiring and negotiation.

**Landed** when the flags about **your** behaviour are as specific as the ones about other people's.
**Did not** when it is a list of things other people do wrong. That version is comfortable and
teaches nothing.

## Self-assessment

```
Include a checklist to score myself against this chapter.
```

**Earns its cost** on skills, character and health.

**Landed** when scoring yourself is unpleasant somewhere. **Did not** when you score high on
everything — which usually means the items were written as aspirations rather than as observations.

## Timeline

```
Include a 7-day and 30-day implementation plan.
```

**Earns its cost** on habits, health and systems.

**Careful with this one.** It sits closest to the failure mode the whole system is built against: a
plan produces the feeling of having started. **Landed** when day 1 is small enough that you would do
it tonight. **Did not** when the plan escalates smoothly to something you have never done, which is
the shape of every plan that gets abandoned in week two.

## Mindset

```
What belief do I need to uninstall, and what replaces it?
```

**Earns its cost** on psychology and identity.

**Landed** when the belief named is one you would defend. **Did not** when it names a belief nobody
holds — *"you may believe you have to do everything alone"* — so the uninstall costs nothing.

## Local

```
Where the chapter's conditions differ from mine — family expectation, Kathmandu, local
norms, economics — ADD a section describing the difference and what it costs. Do not
remove or soften the author's claim, and do not conclude on my behalf that it does not
apply to me.
```

**Earns its cost** on relationships, money, career and status.

**The wording changed on 2026-08-31 and the old version was wrong.** It read *"Adapt fully to
Nepali/Newar context"*, which licenses exactly the subtraction he ruled out: *"you don't remove,
alter the things that author wanted to say for original intent, you can give extra instead."*
`context/reader.md` §4.

**Landed** when the author's claim is still on the page, intact, with the difference beside it.
**Did not** when the chapter has been quietly rewritten into advice for Kathmandu and you can no
longer tell what the book said.

## Numbers

```
Show the actual numbers, calculations and thresholds, adapted to NPR and Nepali market
conditions — and keep the original figures beside them so I can see what changed.
```

**Earns its cost** on finance, health and business.

**Landed** when both sets of figures are visible and the conversion is stated. **Did not** when only
the converted numbers survive: a rule of thumb calibrated to a different economy, silently
re-denominated, is a number that has lost its meaning while keeping its authority.

## Contrarian

```
Give me the strongest case AGAINST this chapter's thesis before the synthesis.
```

**Earns its cost** on **anything you are agreeing with too readily.**

**Landed** when the case is one you have to think about. **Did not** when the objections are all
about scope — *"this may not apply in every industry"* — which is a way of agreeing while appearing
not to.

---

## The one that is not optional

**Contrarian.** Reach for it whenever a chapter is going down easily. Agreement is the default
failure mode of this genre and of this tool at the same time — the book is written to persuade you,
and the model is disposed to agree with whatever you bring it.

If three chapters in a row produced no pushback worth recording, that is a finding about the
transcript, not about the book.

---

## Where a toggle goes, and where it must not

| Add to | Toggles |
|---|---|
| `P3` part B (the rebuild) | Scripts · Cheat sheet · Local · Numbers |
| `P4` (the explanation layer) | Reflection · Flags · Mindset · Contrarian · Local |
| `P6` (candidate actions) | Drills · Decision tree · Self-assessment · Timeline |

**Never add a toggle to `P0`.** It is the standing contract, and a toggle there applies to every
chapter of the book without your noticing — which is how a toggle stops meaning anything.

**No toggle may produce content in `## Recall` or `## Open questions`.** Those are the prohibition
zone, and a toggle that asks the model to draft reflection answers has walked straight into it.

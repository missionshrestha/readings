# Phrasebook — what to actually type

`03-OPERATING-RULES.md` gives the theory. This gives the sentences.

Keep it open for the first few chapters. The difference between the interactions that preserve
understanding and the ones that destroy it is almost entirely **which of these you type** — and
under time pressure the wrong ones come to hand first.

---

## When you want its reading of a chapter

| ✗ Destroys | ✓ Preserves |
|---|---|
| "This chapter argues X, right?" | "What does this chapter argue?" |
| "I think he's saying we should Y — agree?" | "Give me your reading first. I'll give you mine after." |
| "Summarise chapter 4." | "I think chapter 4 argues X. Where am I wrong?" *(after reading it)* |
| "Explain chapter 4." | "Here's what I got from chapter 4. What did I miss?" |

**The whole game is in the first column stating a belief and the second withholding one.** Withhold
the belief and the sycophancy trigger is absent.

---

## When you are about to accept something

> - "Who disagrees with that, and what is their strongest point?"
> - "Is that documented, or observed? Against which edition?"
> - "You agreed with that immediately. Argue the other side properly."
> - "What would someone who has actually tried this and failed say?"
> - "What is the scale this advice assumes? Whose life does it fit?"

**"You agreed with that immediately" is the single most useful sentence in this file.** Use it the
moment a conversation feels comfortable.

---

## When it starts doing your work

> **Stop. That is mine.** Ask me the question instead.

> Do not give me the plan. Give me three candidates with honest impact estimates and let me choose.

> I have not written my recall yet. Do not explain this chapter to me.

---

## When you want the difficulty back

> - "Where does this fail? Give me three specific situations, with what I would actually see."
> - "What is the version of this that would backfire in my context?"
> - "This assumes something about my circumstances. What?"
> - "What does this look like when it fails *quietly*?"

That last one is the most valuable question here. **Quiet failures are the expensive ones**, because
nothing distinguishes them from it working.

---

## When you are choosing an action

> Give me candidates and stop. Do not recommend one.

> Ask me what will actually stop me. Refuse a vague answer.

> It is eight weeks from now and this failed. What happened? Attack the trigger first.

**Never:** "help me stay motivated", "what would success look like", "help me visualise this
working". The last one has evidence directly against it.

---

## The drift test

Every fifth chapter, ask yourself — **not the model** — these two:

1. **In the last five chapters, how many times did I write my recall before reading its
   explanation?** Fewer than four means you have drifted, and the drift is invisible from inside.
2. **Could I say what the last chapter argued, right now, with nothing open?** If not, the recall was
   done as a formality and the chapter is not read.
